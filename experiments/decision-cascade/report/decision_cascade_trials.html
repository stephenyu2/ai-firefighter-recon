Decision Cascade Trials

I tested two new open decision models, d3 (26B) and d3-mini (4.5B), as the fire-status decider for my recon gateway, compared them with every earlier method, and ran them as a cascade that hands low-confidence calls to Qwen3.8-27B. Scored on the 20 test sequences and on every frame of the Florida burn.
By Spencer Theissen-VangExperiment design, model deployment, testing, analysis and writeupCIS 5980, Fall 2026 · October 18, 2026

Summary

I tested d3 and d3-mini, the "Decision 3.0" models from the vLLM Semantic Router project. They read images directly and answer typed questions with a probability for every option, without writing text. I compared them with Kai, Clef, Clef-Flash and the Qwen3.8-27B builds on the same frames and sequences as my earlier gateway model trials.

Then I built a cascade. d3 looks at all 8 frames of a window and makes the fire call when it is confident; otherwise Qwen3.8-27B takes a deeper look. On the 20 test sequences the cascade ties the best earlier method (17 of 20, 4 of 7 small RGB-only fires, no false alarms). On every frame of the Florida burn it called all 101 fire windows and all 22 clean windows correctly, and it processed the whole day of flying in 49.5 minutes. The gap left is small fires with no thermal data: d3 missed 3 of 7 of those with more than 90% confidence, so a confidence gate cannot catch them.

What I recommend

  • Use the cascade with d3 (26B) as the first stage, and keep thermal as the authority whenever a frame has it. d3-mini is too weak on small fires to be the first stage.
  • Give the decision model the images and only strong facts, such as thermal readings. With my RGB detector's counts in the request, d3 missed every small fire.
  • Fix the confident misses at the input before tuning thresholds: the drone frames are 8000 by 6000 pixels and the pipeline currently sends 640 by 512 crops, so a small fire can shrink to a few pixels. Send higher-resolution tiles instead.
  • Treat the 0.80 escalation threshold as provisional until it is checked on footage outside my 20 test sequences.

How the cascade works

In short

d3 reads all 8 frames and decides when its top answer has at least 80% probability. Everything else goes to Qwen3.8-27B for a deeper pass, and the measurement rule, with thermal as the authority, can still raise the call to fire.

How I tested

Single frames
One RGB frame, the question "Are flames or glowing fire visible?", scored on 240 frames and on a fresh sample of 221 that shares none of them. Run with and without the observer guidance, and once more to check that answers repeat.
Test sequences
The same 20 eight-frame sequences I built for the gateway model trials: 8 with thermal, 12 RGB only, 7 of those small fires the RGB detector misses. Florida truth comes from the thermal camera; Sycan truth from the dataset labels.
Request variants
Each decision model got the sequences three ways: measurements as text plus the first and last frame (how Kai and Clef were tested), all 8 frames plus guidance plus measurements, and all 8 frames plus guidance with no measurements.
Full pipeline
Qwen3.8-27B BF16 briefs rerun on all 20 sequences with d3 and d3-mini as in-brief deciders. The rerun matched the earlier writeup calls on 20 of 20.
Cascade
Run on the 20 sequences twice, once with d3 and once with d3-mini as the first stage.
Full burn
Every frame of the Florida burn (FLAME 3 nadir plots, Hanna Hammock), cut into 170 consecutive 8-frame windows per plot and phase. A window is fire if any frame has a thermal pixel at or above 80 C, clean if every frame is a clean negative, and unscored if any frame is unlabeled (post-burn and plot 3).
Ground rules
I called only the new models, Qwen3.8 BF16 and the fire classifier; earlier models are compared from their saved results. I fixed the thresholds before any run. Before first running the d3 and d3-flash package code, I reviewed it line by line and found nothing beyond model code.

What I learned

  • Give the decision model images, and keep weak numbers out of the request. With my RGB detector's counts in the text ("fire in 0 of 8 frames"), d3 missed every small fire. With the images and the guidance alone it caught 2 of 7. TypeSafe's Jev documentation gives the same advice: keep arithmetic in code, and expect accuracy to fall as unrelated content grows.
  • Model size matters for small fires. d3 catches about half of small fires on single frames; d3-flash and d3-mini catch under a tenth. The cheaper models are fine on obvious fires and clean ground, and fail exactly on the hard case.
  • A cascade only helps where the first model knows it is unsure. d3's escalations landed on the hard sequences, and the deeper pass got them right. Its three misses were confident, so no threshold would have caught them.
  • The confident misses are most likely a resolution problem. The Florida frames are 8000 by 6000 pixels; the pipeline sends 640 by 512 crops, and d3 reads images in 32 by 32 pixel patches. A fire a few pixels wide in the original can fall below one patch. Studies of vision models find accuracy is highly sensitive to how large the subject is in the image.
  • Guidance text works differently per model. The same observer guidance raised Clef 27B from 0.32 to 0.55 single-frame recall and Qwen3.8 BF16 from 0.40 to 0.53, but left d3 almost unchanged. d3 shows each option to the model as "name: description", so guidance may work better inside the option descriptions than appended to the question.
  • Thermal still decides when it exists. On the full burn every during-burn window had thermal data, and the cascade was right on all of them, including 6 where d3 alone said "no fire".
  • Each question costs a full pass over the images. The cascade asks 2 questions and its d3 call took a median 2.3 s for 8 frames; the 4-question sequence test took about 11 s. Ask only what the decision needs.
  • Serving details move speed, not answers. The two d3 setups agreed frame for frame, but the RTX PRO 6000 setup, with the optional linear-attention kernels, ran 8-frame requests about 3 times faster than the H100 setup without them.
  • Runs are reproducible. Every decision model gave identical answers on repeat, and rerunning the full Qwen3.8 pipeline reproduced the earlier calls on 20 of 20 sequences.

Next steps

  • Tiles at higher resolution. Cut each original 8000 by 6000 frame into tiles, ask d3 about each, and take the highest fire probability. This needs no tuning, so it can be scored fairly on the same 20 sequences.
  • Smaller, sharper questions. Ask separately about flame or glow, smoke rising from a ground source, and a fresh burn edge, each with explicit yes and no descriptions, and combine the answers in code.
  • Correct for option order. d3 always lists "no" first on yes or no questions. Ask the fire-status question twice with the options reversed and average the two.
  • Test the RGB-only path at burn scale. Rerun the plot 2 burn with thermal withheld (65 windows), the case where the cascade still misses fires.
  • Set the escalation threshold on new footage. Choose it on burns outside these 20 sequences, then freeze it.

Method notes

  • The 20-sequence comparison comes down to 7 small RGB-only fires, so a difference of one sequence is within noise.
  • "Measurement rule can raise fire" reuses the rule from my gateway model trials. On RGB-only footage its false-alarm check rests on the 4 no-fire test sequences from those trials. In the full burn, d3 decided all 21 RGB-only pre-burn windows as no fire on its own, so the rule was not exercised there.
  • The full burn mainly tests the thermal path: every fire window had thermal data, and the measurement rule never had to raise a call on RGB alone.
  • The cascade's 0.80 threshold and 2 retries were set before any run and not tuned. No retries were needed in any run.
  • Earlier models (Kai, Clef, Clef-Flash, other Qwen3.8 builds) were not rerun; their numbers come from my gateway model trials, on the same frames and sequences.
  • Data: FLAME 3 (Hopkins et al., 2024, arXiv 2412.02831) and the FLAME 3 nadir thermal plot subset (Tall Timbers Research Station, Hanna Hammock burn).
